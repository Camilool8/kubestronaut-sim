**Organizations that run cloud native technologies in production to operate their own business, rather than building and selling cloud native products** is correct: the End User Community is a distinct CNCF membership category from vendor members. It exists so that companies USING projects like Kubernetes and Prometheus in production — rather than companies building commercial offerings around them — have a direct channel to share real operational feedback with maintainers and influence project direction and the TOC's priorities.

Why the others are wrong:

- **Companies that sell managed Kubernetes distributions or cloud native SaaS products** — that describes vendor members, the complementary membership category the End User Community is explicitly separate from.
- **Individual open source contributors who are not affiliated with any company** — individual contributor status is a matter of a project's own CONTRIBUTING/governance docs, not the organizational "end user member" category.
- **The maintainers of CNCF-hosted projects** — maintainers are the people who develop and govern a given project's codebase; end user members are the organizations consuming projects downstream, a different role entirely.
